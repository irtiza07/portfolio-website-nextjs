It's been 4 long years since I last wrote [*The Best Productivity System You Can Use For $0*](https://irtizahafiz.medium.com/the-best-productivity-system-you-can-use-for-0-fea699e53cdf).

Although I never personally adopted an all-Google productivity system, with the recent AI hype, I have been tempted to see the power of Google Gemini in the context of productivity. And boy I was surprised!

Google Gemini can supercharge any productivity system that uses some combination of Google products - Google Calendar, Tasks, Sheets, Docs, Gmail, Photos, etc.

If the system is built right, it can drastically reduce screen time, because all you do is interact with Gemini. You don't need to individually open each app or sync changes across all of them.

You can achieve all this and more for exactly $0.

So, let's get started.

## The Fundamentals

I spent most of last week building systems and workflows that migrate my data from non-Google apps to Google.

- Google Calendar - All of life's most important events and meetings go here. Previously, I used TickTick as a calendar.
- Google Tasks - Day to day tasks, random errands, and project specific tasks. Previously, I used TickTick.
- Gmail - pretty simple, emails. Previously, I used a combination of Gmail and Protonmail. Now, all Protonmail emails are automatically forwarded to my primary Gmail inbox.
- Google Photos - cloud storage of all photos. Previously, I used Ente Photos.
- Google Docs - note taking and writing app of choice. Previously, I used Obsidian.
- Google Sheets - content roadmaps, budget trackers, net worth trackers, quarterly goals, etc. Previously, I used a combination of Proton Sheets and TickTick.
- Google Drive - files directory. Previously, I used Proton Drive.

Then there are other apps like Google Keep, but I don't consider them essentials.

Once all the data is within this Google Ecosystem, you put Gemini on top, and you see magic happen.

Gemini can pull context from the Google Ecosystem to answer any question you want. Let's get into those next.

## Google Gemini

Gemini is embedded into all Google products and devices. Think of it as your personal assistant.

Once Gemini is connected to Google Workspace (which requires one tap/click), it can figure out which apps to pull data from based on your question or query. Seldom, I need to directly ask Gemini to look at my inbox or photos. Instead, I keep my question focused on the ask, not the retrieval method.

Let me take you through a few examples.

### "What's on my agenda tomorrow?"

Google pulls data from Tasks and Calendar to give you a list of everything coming up the next day.

Not only that, but Google also scans recent emails and conversation chats to surface time-sensitive follow ups.

### "How much did I pay for the new brakes?"

Google searches your email inbox for receipts, parses the receipt, then gives you an itemized breakdown of each expense.

You can even follow up with something like - "When do I have to go for my next oil change?" - and Google can find the information from your email correspondence or receipt itself.

It gets even better - once you have the date, you can ask Gemini to create a Google Task or Google Calendar event.

Previously, this whole sequence required me to go to 2-3 separate apps for manual data entry.

### "Summarize my emails from last week."

This one's straightforward, but very powerful.

Of course Gemini can give you a condensed summary, but better yet, it can extract tasks (that can be turned into a Google Task or Google Calendar event) and consolidate newsletters and other FYI emails into a nice bullet point list.

### "Timeblock tomorrow's calendar"

Just by naturally communicating with Gemini, you can give it a sense of your work patterns, daily habits, meal schedule, etc.

Then, when you ask Gemini to timeblock or structure your day, it does the following:

- Pulls calendar events from Google Calendar
- Pulls tasks from Google Tasks
- Remembers when you like doing your deep focus work
- Remembers your meal times
- Remembers how long certain types of tasks take
- Proposes a schedule
- Once confirmed, updates all tasks and calendar events with the relevant date/time

I used to go through these exact steps on TickTick, but now everything is so much more natural.

### "Remind me to renew my driver's license next Monday. Also look up the exact location and relevant forms I need to fill."

Gemini does the following:

- Creates a calendar event
- Uses Google Maps to find nearby Drivers Services offices
- Uses Web Search to make a list of required documents
- Adds the location and required documents to the calendar invite

Things like these cut down a lot of prep time.

Yes, Gemini is still prone to mistakes, so I need to double check some of the details. However, starting from 60% is better than 0%.

### "I am not sure where to park for tomorrow's Falcon game. Find 3 nearby parking options with prices and hours."

Gemini does the following:

- Finds the best parking options
- Creates a Google Maps link for each
- Finds the relevant calendar event
- Adds parking options to the event description

With these done in the background, I only have to open Google Calendar on my phone game day, then pick the best parking option for Google Maps to help navigate.

This is a great example of how AI can reduce screen time, not increase it.

## The Pricing

You can easily create a system like this for $0.

The free tier of Gemini is smart enough for most queries. There's a limit on usage, but I am yet to hit that, even though I am putting it through its paces the last 2 weeks.

The primary cost comes from storage.

Google gives you 15GB free storage. However, that storage is shared between all apps - Gmail, Drive, Photos, etc.

Depending on how many files and photos you plan to store, you can easily exceed the 15GB tier.

In that case, you'll have to pay for storage. However, odds are you would pay that regardless, whether you used Gemini or not. Most cloud storages cost money beyond the entry-level 15GB or 30GB tiers.
